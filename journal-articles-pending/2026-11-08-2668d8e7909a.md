# Informational Leadership: How Workplace Climate Shapes Digital Transformation

Digital transformation requires more than just technology; it requires leaders who can navigate data. A new study examines how personality and environment shape this capability.

### Introduction

In the era of digital transformation, informational leadership has become a critical competency for effective institutional governance. This capability involves the strategic use of data in decision-making and the implementation of digital systems.

A new study published in the Journal of Education and Learning Reviews (DOI: 10.60027/jelr.2026.e2985) examines how administrators' personality traits and organizational climate jointly shape this type of leadership.

### What the study examined

According to the abstract, the research focused on private higher education institutions in Thailand facing intense competitive pressures. The goal was to determine if organizational climate mediates the relationship between administrators' personality traits and their ability to lead digital agendas.

The authors employed a quantitative cross-sectional design, analyzing data from 350 administrators. Personality traits were measured using the Five-Factor Model, and organizational climate was assessed within a digital governance framework.

### Findings and interpretation

According to the abstract, personality traits significantly influenced informational leadership (β = 0.39). However, a significant finding is the partial mediation: after accounting for organizational climate, the direct effect decreased to 0.23, while the indirect effect through climate was 0.18.

Organizational climate exerted the strongest influence (β = 0.54) with a large effect size. The model explained 55% of the variance in informational leadership. Interestingly, senior administrators were more influenced by their disposition, whereas middle-level management relied more heavily on a supportive climate for digital enactment.

### Methodology and limitations

The research utilized Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) and Confirmatory Factor Analysis. Mediation was tested using bias-corrected bootstrapping with 5,000 resamples, which enhances the robustness of the statistical estimates.

It is important to note that this is an abstract of the study, which does not provide the full context of the cultural specifics of the Thai education sector. Consequently, the results cannot be automatically generalized to other industries or regions without further verification.

### WEBU practical proposal

For business owners and web teams, these findings suggest that investing in digital tools is not enough. To improve data-driven decision-making, you must build a culture that supports innovation rather than just searching for the 'ideal' leader.

In practice, when implementing new web systems or CRM platforms, you should actively support middle management in open communication. Create an environment where data-related errors are viewed as learning opportunities rather than failures, thereby strengthening their informational leadership.

### Conclusion

The study confirms that informational leadership is not merely an innate trait but is significantly amplified by the environment. For institutions, this implies the need for a dual strategy: leadership development combined with systematic climate-building initiatives.

While the results from the Thai context are specific, the core insight—that organizational climate is a key multiplier of digital capabilities—remains a universal lesson for modern management.
