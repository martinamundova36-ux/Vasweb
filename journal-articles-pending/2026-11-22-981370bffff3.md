According to the abstract, the study provides an empirical basis for understanding why some countries lag in innovation adoption and what the main obstacles are for enterprises operating in less developed digital ecosystems within the European Union.

The study examined the integration of six specific technologies: enterprise resource planning (ERP), customer relationship management (CRM), business intelligence, cloud computing, artificial intelligence, and data analytics. The data are derived from Eurostat surveys on information and communication technology usage in enterprises for the period 2019–2025.

The research identified a clear leading group consisting of four countries, while six Central and Eastern European countries, including Romania, make up the low-adoption group. These results suggest that the mere availability of technology is insufficient; the key factor for successful digital transformation is the ability of employees and managers to effectively master these tools and implement them into business processes in real time.

The study's methodology included descriptive statistics, sigma and beta convergence analysis, a composite integration index, and hierarchical cluster analysis. According to the abstract, these methods were used to process data from Eurostat surveys. The study's limitations stem from the nature of secondary data and the focus on the macro level. According to the abstract, the study does not provide an in-depth qualitative view of why specific firms fail in AI adoption.
