Modern markets demand high levels of transparency and social responsibility from companies. A new study published in the International Journal of Organizational Analysis examines how perceived corporate ethics shapes customer engagement and loyalty.

### Introduction

Organisational ethics has become a vital factor in shaping stakeholder relationships. Today, consumers expect brands to deliver not only quality products but also demonstrable transparency and social responsibility.

Although prior research has addressed ethical perceptions across various branding contexts, less attention has been paid to how individual consumer characteristics shape these relationships in emerging markets.

### What the study examined

According to the abstract, the study examines how perceived organisational ethics influences engagement and loyalty within the food industry, using Alpina as the empirical context.

The authors Nathalie Peña-García, Mauricio Losada-Otálora, and Iván D. Sánchez also investigated whether these relationships vary across different educational levels of consumers.

### Findings and interpretation

According to the abstract, perceived organisational ethics significantly influences brand engagement and perceived value, which in turn predict customer loyalty. Customer experience and trust also shape how consumers interpret the organisation's positioning.
