# Weighed by Intelligence: How AI Shapes Green Innovations in Firms

Recent research published in Management of Environmental Quality provides a fresh perspective on how artificial intelligence shapes corporate green innovations. Drawing on an attention-based view, the authors examine the dual nature of technology in achieving environmental objectives.

### Introduction

Digital transformation and sustainability remain core pillars of modern business strategy. The intersection of artificial intelligence and environmental innovation introduces new managerial complexities in balancing technological advancements with ecological responsibilities.

A new study by authors Congcong Fan, Fan Zhang, and Chenhuan Fan investigates this precise phenomenon, offering theoretical insights and practical implications for contemporary organizations.

### What the study examined

According to the abstract, this study examines how artificial intelligence adoption shapes firms' ambidextrous green innovation by inducing differential effects on exploratory and exploitative activities from an attention-based perspective.

Using panel data from Chinese listed firms and employing regression analyses, the researchers sought to understand how technological tools reconfigure internal organizational processes and attention allocation.

### Findings and interpretation

According to the abstract, AI adoption exerts a double-edged effect on ambidextrous green innovation. It significantly promotes exploratory green innovation by redirecting managerial attention toward forward-looking strategic issues.

Conversely, according to the abstract, AI suppresses exploitative green innovation by institutionalizing routine processes and reducing sustained attention to incremental improvements. Furthermore, Confucian cultural embeddedness, negative media coverage, and institutional ownership are stated in the abstract to strengthen AI's positive effect on exploratory innovation while mitigating its negative effect on exploitative innovation.
