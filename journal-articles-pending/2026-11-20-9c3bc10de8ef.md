# Digital Leadership and Sustainability in SMEs

This study examines how digital leadership influences the sustainability performance of small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) through FinTech adoption and organizational agility. Analyzing data from 1,039 senior officials across three countries, the research highlights that the path to sustainability is not universal but depends heavily on the specific institutional environment and the role of responsible innovation.

### Introduction

Digital transformation has emerged as a cornerstone of modern business, with its impact on environmental and social sustainability becoming a subject of intense academic scrutiny. According to the abstract, this study focuses on how leaders in SMEs leverage digital tools to achieve long-term sustainability goals. The research seeks to understand the mechanisms that connect business leadership with specific technological and organizational capabilities within diverse national contexts.

The authors integrate the 'Upper Echelons' theory and the Dynamic Capabilities theory to explain how strategic decisions influence firm performance. According to the abstract, the goal is to identify whether there is a direct path from digital leadership to sustainability or if mediating factors, such as FinTech adoption and organizational agility, are required. This analysis provides valuable insights for entrepreneurs attempting to align digital strategy with sustainable development.

### What the study examined

The research focused on 1,039 senior officials across three distinct countries: Pakistan (472 respondents), Malaysia (303 respondents), and Saudi Arabia (264 respondents). According to the abstract, the study examined the sequential mediating roles of digital leadership in FinTech adoption and organizational agility. It also focused on how responsible innovation moderates these relationships within different institutional architectures, aiming to determine whether these processes are universally applicable across various economic settings.

The study analyzed how digital leadership fosters FinTech adoption and whether this adoption subsequently increases organizational agility, leading to improved sustainability. According to the abstract, emphasis was placed on how responsible innovations influence these pathways. It is important to note that the study was not limited to one specific business sector but covered a broad sample of SMEs, which allows for a wider perspective on digital transformation under varying economic conditions.

### Findings and interpretation

The findings, according to the abstract, reveal that the mechanism of digital leadership's impact on sustainability is not universal. In Pakistan, an inconsistent mediation pattern was observed, where a positive indirect effect through FinTech coexisted with a negative direct effect, resulting in a non-significant total effect. Conversely, in Malaysia and Saudi Arabia, only indirect-only mediation occurred, where FinTech and organizational agility effectively mediated the relationship between leadership and sustainability.

Responsible innovation plays different roles according to the abstract: in Pakistan, it negatively moderated the path between agility and sustainability, while in Malaysia and Saudi Arabia, it positively moderated the path between leadership and FinTech adoption. These differences suggest that the national institutional environment fundamentally reconfigures strategic business pathways. The findings emphasize that entrepreneurs must account for local conditions, as strategies that work in one country may not yield the same results elsewhere.

### Methodology and limitations

The research employed a two-wave longitudinal design and PLS-SEM analysis. According to the abstract, data collection occurred over one year, allowing for the observation of changes over time. However, as this is an abstract, detailed statistical characteristics of the sample and precise definitions of 'sustainability' used in the questionnaires are unknown. These limitations mean that results should be interpreted with caution, particularly regarding causality, which this type of design may not fully establish.

Another limitation according to the abstract is that the study focused on three specific countries, which limits the generalizability of the results to other regions, such as Europe. Furthermore, the absence of information regarding the industrial focus of the firms means that sector-specific factors could have influenced the results. Entrepreneurs should note that the study provides a strategic framework rather than a universal manual, with institutional differences between countries playing a key role in the success of digital strategies.

### Business relevance and limitations

Entrepreneurs should understand that digital transformation is not just about technology, but about adapting to the local institutional environment. According to the abstract, it is crucial to consider how FinTech adoption and building organizational agility work together to achieve sustainability. For example, if an entrepreneur operates in a market with dynamics similar to Malaysia, they could test whether investments in FinTech tools directly support their team's agility, and subsequently measure whether this agility manifests in better environmental or social firm outcomes.

It is important to monitor whether innovations in the firm are truly 'responsible' and contribute to sustainability, or if they might be counterproductive in certain contexts.
