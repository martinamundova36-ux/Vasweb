# From Forecast Accuracy to Resilience: The Impact of Artificial Intelligence on Supply Chain Management

This study examines how artificial intelligence transforms supply chain management through four key mechanisms. ### Introduction

Artificial intelligence is increasingly presented as a general-purpose technology capable of solving complex challenges in supply chain management. Despite this enthusiasm, existing academic literature remains fragmented, focusing on various technology families and functional areas without a unified framework. This research aims to bridge this gap and provide an integrative account of how AI changes not only decision-making processes but also the overall outcomes for businesses within global supply chain networks.

According to the abstract, the study analyzes how AI integrates into operations and how it influences business performance. The goal is to understand whether AI truly replaces human planners or rather changes their role and focus of attention. For entrepreneurs, it is crucial to understand that technology is not a panacea but a tool whose benefit is conditional on specific organizational and structural prerequisites that firms must build to fully leverage automation and analytics.

### What the study examined

The study focused on identifying four main mechanisms through which AI affects supply chains. According to the abstract, these include richer demand signal extraction and forecasting, automated evaluation and monitoring of suppliers and contracts, dynamic optimization of inventory, production and logistics decisions, and finally, earlier detection of and faster response to disruptions. These mechanisms form the core of the technological intervention into traditional processes that were previously dependent primarily on manual inputs and static methods.

In addition to these mechanisms, the research examined the so-called ladder of delegated authority, which ranges from simple descriptive analytics to bounded autonomous decision-making. According to the abstract, the realization of these functions depends on data infrastructure, organizational absorptive capacity, and governance. The study seeks to explain that technology itself, without adequate organizational backing, cannot deliver expected results, which is an important finding for managers considering investments in AI solutions within their logistics operations.

### Findings and interpretation

According to the abstract, reported performance effects of AI are positive but conditional. Survey evidence associates AI-enabled analytics with better operational and environmental performance. On the other hand, benchmarking evidence shows that machine learning does not automatically outperform established statistical methods. This finding is crucial as it refutes the myth of the immediate superiority of AI over traditional approaches, emphasizing the need for critical evaluation of every implemented technology in the context of a company's specific needs.

The principal impact of AI, according to the abstract, is less the substitution of human planners than the compression of decision latency and the reallocation of managerial attention. Technology enables faster data processing, allowing managers to focus on more strategic tasks instead of routine information processing. This shift in attention is essential for increasing supply chain resilience, as it allows organizations to respond to market changes with greater agility and precision than was possible with traditional planning methods.

The study draws on nine conceptual, empirical, and review sources published between 1997 and 2024. According to the abstract, it is an integrative approach that attempts to synthesize fragmented knowledge from various fields. It is important to note that the abstract does not provide detailed information about sample sizes, statistical tests, or whether the data were collected primarily or secondarily. This lack of detail limits the possibility of independently verifying the robustness of the conclusions and requires caution when interpreting results across different industries.

Limitations include the fact that the abstract does not specify which particular types of companies or industries were represented in the sources. According to the abstract, the realization of AI depends on organizational capacity, meaning that results may not be generalizable to small and medium-sized enterprises with limited resources. Since the study combines different types of sources, there is a risk that findings may be influenced by the heterogeneity of methodologies used in the original works, making it difficult to determine causal relationships between AI and performance.

### Business relevance and limitations

Entrepreneurs should view AI as a tool to reduce decision latency, not as a replacement for strategic thinking. According to the abstract, success is conditional on data infrastructure and organizational capacity. Before investing in AI, it is essential to audit the quality of internal data and the team's ability to interpret outputs from analytical tools. If data is not clean and the organization is not prepared for process change, even the most advanced algorithm will not bring the expected improvement in operational efficiency.

For example, if an entrepreneur is considering introducing AI for demand forecasting, they should first test the accuracy of existing statistical methods compared to the new solution. They should measure not only the accuracy of the forecast but also the time managers spend adjusting these forecasts. It is important to track whether AI actually shortens the time needed for inventory ordering decisions. This approach will help distinguish the real benefit of the technology from the marketing promises of software vendors, while also accounting for data maintenance costs.

### Conclusion

The research provides valuable insight that AI in the supply chain is primarily about changing managerial attention and reaction speed. According to the abstract, the key to success is the integration of technology into existing processes with an emphasis on organizational readiness. Technology alone does not guarantee better results unless it is supported by quality data and proper management, which allows for the effective use of outputs from analytical models.

In conclusion, companies that want to leverage AI must invest not only in software but also in developing the skills of their employees. According to the abstract, it is necessary to view AI as part of a wider ecosystem where technology, data, and human judgment work together to achieve higher resilience. For entrepreneurs, this means that the path to success leads through gradual implementation and constant verification of benefits in real operational conditions, rather than blind trust in automated solutions.
