# Digital Transformation in Developing Economies: What a New Study Says About Industry 4.0

A new study published in an international journal provides insight into how firms in developing economies leverage modern technologies. We examine its findings, limitations, and editorial recommendations for small businesses.

### Introduction

Current technological trends often assume ideal conditions, advanced infrastructure, and abundant capital. In practice, however, many regions worldwide, including developing economies, face severe structural constraints, skill shortages, and financial barriers.

A new study by Erick Calvo, Mario Ferrer, and M. Sirajul Islam, published on November 17, 2026 (DOI: 10.1108/ijppm-06-2026-0660), addresses the strategic value of Industry 4.0 technologies. It examines their deployment within the specific setting of Colombia's North Coast.

This article provides an editorial perspective on the research, relying strictly on the publisher-supplied abstract. We review the data collected, the study's limitations, and the implications for practice.

### What the study examined

According to the abstract, the research investigates the strategic value of Industry 4.0 technologies in developing economies, where empirical evidence remains limited. The authors specifically focused on robotics, additive manufacturing, and the Internet of Things (IoT).

The objective was to assess how firms deploy these technologies to improve supply chain and organizational performance under structural constraints such as limited infrastructure, skill shortages, and financial restrictions.

Grounding their work in the resource-based view, the authors reconceptualized the valuable, rare, inimitable, and nonsubstitutable (VRIN) status as an emergent property of the adoption context rather than a fixed technology attribute.

### Findings and interpretation

According to the abstract, data collection yielded specific results regarding individual technologies. Robotics and additive manufacturing were found to significantly enhance supply chain efficiency and, in turn, organizational performance.

Conversely, the Internet of Things (IoT) showed no significant effect on supply chain efficiency. According to the text, this reflects integration challenges and infrastructural limitations within the region.

This highlights the study's core insight: the value of Industry 4.0 technologies is context-dependent, shaped by local capability and resource conditions. Technologies function as strategic resources only when contextual conditions permit.

### Methodology and limitations

According to the abstract, data were collected from 125 supply chain managers in manufacturing firms through a structured online survey. Structural equation modeling (SEM) was employed to analyze the relationships among variables.

Despite its valuable insights, the study has clear limitations that must be acknowledged. According to the abstract, the research is restricted to one region of Colombia and relies solely on managerial perceptions.

It is important to emphasize that this relies on a secondary-access abstract, as the complete full-text article was not verified. Consequently, broad causal claims cannot be made, nor can results be uncritically applied to different geographic or market settings.

### Conclusion

The new study by Calvo, Ferrer, and Islam serves as a reminder that modern technologies alone do not guarantee success without adequate local conditions and capabilities. The findings reveal a clear divergence between the impact of robotics and the challenges of IoT implementation.

For WEBU readers, the key takeaway is the necessity of critically assessing any technological trend. Before investing in digital upgrades, evaluating real operational and financial constraints is essential.

Future research will need to validate these findings through longitudinal and multi-regional designs. Until then, managers and web operators should proceed prudently and build complementary capabilities step by step.
