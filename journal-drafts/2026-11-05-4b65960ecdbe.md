## English version

# From engagement to loyalty: why brand trust matters

## Introduction

A high number of social media reactions does not necessarily mean a customer will return. Small businesses need to understand whether people trust the offer, find its information credible and have reasons to choose the service again.

## What the study examined

The paper by Akroush and colleagues addresses engagement, loyalty, brand trust and brand social media posts, as indicated by its title. Its bibliographic record identifies the Journal of Product & Brand Management. The research design, sample and analytic model still require verification against the full text.

## Findings and interpretation

The title refers to mediating roles of trust and posts, but this is not evidence of a particular effect size. Before reporting results we need to verify how each construct was measured and whether the study supports associations rather than causal mechanisms. Sales-growth promises would be premature.

## Methodology and limitations

Interpretation must distinguish self-reported loyalty from observed repeat purchases and account for the sample and sector. It would be inappropriate to assume the same pattern in a restaurant, wellness service and online shop without further evidence.

## WEBU practical proposal

WEBU suggests piloting three elements: consistent service descriptions across the website and social channels, clear booking conditions and accurate answers to customer questions. Track qualified enquiries, completed bookings and repeat contact over several weeks; treat post engagement as a supporting metric only. This is editorial guidance, not a tested intervention from the paper.

## Conclusion

Trust is a valuable business question, not a guaranteed sales formula. Precise study findings should be added only after the full article has been checked.
