Customer Retention in D2C E-Commerce: An Integrated Framework and Its Boundaries

Author: Martina Mundová

Direct-to-consumer (D2C) sales present enormous challenges regarding customer loyalty. A new study published in November 2026 introduces a conceptual framework bridging expectation-confirmation theory, trust, and customer lifetime value. We examine what this text actually claims and where its limits lie.

Introduction

Digital channels in modern e-commerce provide brands with countless opportunities to interact with consumers. However, mere online presence does not automatically guarantee that customers will return and make repeat purchases. Building lasting loyalty in a direct-to-consumer (D2C) environment thus becomes a critical factor for the long-term sustainability of online stores.

Authors Sarvesh Bamnodkar, Shreya Bhujbal, and Dr. Padmashree Chandak published a study in November 2026 in the International Journal of Creative and Open Research in Engineering and Management (DOI: 10.55041/ijcope.v2i11.030). This work focuses on establishing an integrated framework for customer retention and analyzes factors influencing repeat purchases.

In this article, we examine what the study presents, its theoretical foundations, and the methodological limitations of the research. We also connect these academic insights with concrete editorial recommendations for owners of small and medium web projects.

What the study examined

According to the abstract, the study addressed the challenges associated with customer retention in the D2C e-commerce environment. The authors state that while digital channels facilitate repeated interactions, they do not by themselves ensure repeat purchasing or durable loyalty. Topics such as customer journeys, online engagement, and loyalty programmes have already been examined in prior research, including a recent 2026 meta-analysis.

The main purpose of the paper is therefore not to claim that integrating these topics is inherently novel. According to the abstract, the paper develops a measurement-aware conceptual framework that distinguishes lifecycle-oriented marketing activities, customer-response mechanisms, and retention-related outcomes.

In developing this model, the authors drew on the customer-journey perspective, expectation-confirmation theory, commitment-trust theory, customer-engagement scholarship, and customer lifetime value research. The goal was to clarify how these constructs relate to one another.

Findings and interpretation

According to the abstract, the publication does not specify any empirical testing of the proposed relationships, as it is primarily a conceptual work. The significance of the study lies not in statistically proving hypotheses on a large sample, but in systematically organizing theoretical approaches into a coherent framework.

The authors also identify contextual conditions that should shape the evaluation of retention success itself. This means managers and strategy creators should avoid universal templates and instead account for the specific characteristics of their target audience and shopping cycle stage.

The findings suggest that combining commitment-trust theory and expectation-confirmation theory provides a better understanding of why customers abandon a brand after an initial purchase. This serves as a useful theoretical foundation for further research in digital commerce.

Methodology and limitations

It is important to emphasize that this work has clearly defined boundaries. According to the abstract, the authors used a focused, purposive synthesis of selected scholarly sources identified through targeted publisher, repository, and DOI searches, as well as reference-list leads.

The study explicitly is not an exhaustive systematic review of the literature. Furthermore, a crucial limit applies: the proposed relationships and connections have not been empirically tested on real data or customer samples.

The text also uses no confidential company records or private customer data. When interpreting the conclusions of this article or the study itself, one must consider that this is a conceptual model requiring additional practical verification.

WEBU practical proposal

Based on our editorial reflection of the study's theoretical foundations, we offer several practical recommendations for small business owners and e-commerce operators. The first step is to review the post-purchase section—online stores often ignore customers immediately after dispatch, even though confirming expectations through useful content and support at that moment is crucial.

In terms of design and user experience (UX), we recommend simplifying access to order history and loyalty benefits directly within the user account. If customers must laboriously search for past purchase information, their trust and willingness to return diminish.

For management and marketing teams, this means shifting from short-term discount campaigns to building long-term trust. Set up tracking for customer lifetime value (CLV) metrics and invest in personalized communication instead of mass spam.

Conclusion

The study published in November 2026 offers an interesting and useful theoretical perspective on customer retention in D2C e-commerce. It demonstrates that success in the digital environment relies not on random transactions, but on connecting trust, expectations, and a proper understanding of the consumer lifecycle.

At the same time, it is essential to acknowledge the limits of this text. Because it is a conceptual study without empirical testing, its conclusions serve as a conceptual foundation rather than a universal manual with guaranteed results for every business type.

We hope this overview helps you better evaluate academic literature critically and apply proven principles to your own web and marketing strategies.
