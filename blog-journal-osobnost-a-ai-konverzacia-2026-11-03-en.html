AI chat style: evidence from a small controlled study

By WEBU · Published 2026-11-03 · Updated 2026-11-04

Slovenčina

A brief answer may suit a routine customer question, while a complex decision may need more detail. That does not justify inferring a psychological profile from a few messages. Abukar and colleagues (2026) examined how task, assistant style and user experience relate.

What the study examined

We compared the full author preprint arXiv:2608.11164, posted on 11 August 2026. Analysis retained 26 of 28 participants and 78 usable conversations. This was a convenience sample, largely young, male and computing-oriented. Participants’ country of residence is not unambiguously documented in the reviewed methods; the authors’ Glasgow affiliation does not establish it.

In a counterbalanced within-subject design, each participant completed three tasks. Assistants used the same base GPT-4.1 model with prompts expressing extraverted, conscientious or neutral styles. Participants’ Big Five traits were measured, not manipulated. Data included logs, behavioural traces, questionnaires and brief comments.

Main findings

The findings did not identify a universally best style. Preferences and trust ratings varied with the task–assistant combination. Some links between participant traits and behaviour remained directional, particularly after correction for multiple testing.

Experimentally manipulated style in this setting is distinct from correlations involving measured participant personality. The study does not establish that automatic customer profiling improves sales, or that a more likeable answer is more factually accurate.

Putting it into practice — WEBU proposal

Offer a clear choice between brief and detailed responses, plus access to a person. Test that choice within a concrete customer task. This proposal is not a complete system validated by the cited study.

  • Keep response style separate from factual checking.
  • Do not treat a few messages as personality assessment.
  • Measure task completion and errors, not just satisfaction or turn counts.

Research limits and publication status

A small convenience sample, one model and prompt-based style manipulation limit generalisation. Questionnaire trust is not objective accuracy; more conversation turns do not necessarily mean better performance. Not every composite is an independently validated psychological scale.

We cite the preprint actually read. The ACM conference record is dated 6 November 2026 in Crossref, which is still future-dated at this revision; we do not present it as an independently checked final publication. Comparing a preprint with our commentary is not independent peer review.

Conclusion

Investigate style alongside the task and user needs. A small study cannot justify a universal psychological customer profile or a business guarantee.
