The Impact of Online Customer Reviews on Consumer Trust on TikTok: Insights from Recent Research

Author: WEBU

The ecosystem of social commerce continues to expand rapidly, with platforms like TikTok seamlessly integrating shopping features into daily content consumption. A recent study published in the Lead Journal of Economy and Administration investigates how customer reviews shape consumer trust during online purchases. This article examines these findings while carefully noting the study's limitations and offering actionable editorial recommendations for web developers and business owners.

Introduction

The digital landscape introduces novel shopping modalities where social media networks serve far beyond mere communication channels, operating instead as fully functional retail spaces. Blending entertaining content with direct commerce creates a distinct environment for both brands and consumers. Trust within this digital sphere remains paramount, as the inability to inspect a product physically before purchase requires alternative validation methods.

Among the most critical factors guiding user decision-making are the experiences shared by fellow customers in the form of ratings and reviews. These digital footprints form a collective community memory that directly shapes the perceived credibility of a merchant. Observing this phenomenon is essential for understanding contemporary purchasing behavior in the era of social commerce.

What the study examined

The published work by Rohmaniati Padang et al. (2026) aimed to analyze the effect of online customer reviews on consumer trust when shopping through the TikTok application. According to the abstract, the research specifically focused on a sample of students at the Institut Bisnis dan Komputer Indonesia who actively use the platform and have completed purchases via TikTok Shop.

The authors sought to explore associations between these variables within this specific demographic framework. The investigation attempted to understand the degree to which peer evaluations function as a decisive informational resource for the younger generation of digital shoppers.

Findings and interpretation

According to the abstract, the study employed a quantitative approach alongside a simple linear regression method to analyze data collected from 25 respondents chosen through purposive sampling. Primary data acquisition utilized an online questionnaire based on a five-point Likert scale, establishing the methodological foundation for evaluating the connection between reviews and trust.

The results outlined in the abstract demonstrated that online customer reviews exerted a positive and significant effect on consumer trust, marked by a t-value of 5.983 and a significance level of p < 0.05. Furthermore, the coefficient of determination (R²) reported at 0.6088 indicates that online reviews account for over sixty percent of the variation in consumer trust, with the remaining portion driven by external factors.

Methodology and limitations

Evaluating any academic research requires careful consideration of its methodological boundaries and inherent limitations. According to the available abstract, the study relies on a relatively modest sample size of 25 respondents drawn from a specific cohort of university students at a single institution. Such parameters preclude generalizing these findings to broader populations, diverse geographies, or alternate cultural contexts.

Additionally, it is important to note that access is restricted to the publisher-supplied abstract. This constraint prevents a thorough examination of the complete manuscript, verification of the full-text findings, auditing of control variables, or deeper scrutiny of potential data collection biases.

WEBU practical proposal

Building upon general marketing principles and digital behavior insights, we provide editorial recommendations for small business owners and web creators. E-commerce operators should proactively integrate verified customer reviews directly onto product pages, as transparent peer feedback may strengthen trust among visitors encountering a brand for the first time.

When approaching web design, we recommend positioning ratings prominently and ensuring that reviews incorporate visual elements or authentic user-submitted photos. In the domains of marketing and workplace management, businesses benefit from training support teams to handle review feedback diligently, address inquiries promptly, and transform critical remarks into opportunities to demonstrate responsive customer service.

Conclusion

Examining the influence of online reviews on consumer trust highlights the profound power of social proof in the modern digital marketplace. The authors document a statistically significant relationship between evaluations and consumer trust among student participants using TikTok Shop, thereby contributing valuable dialogue to the field of social commerce.

Although the study exhibits clear limitations stemming from its sample size and restricted full-text availability, its core takeaway remains highly relevant. Maintaining transparency and centering authentic customer experiences are fundamental practices for achieving long-term success in online commerce.
