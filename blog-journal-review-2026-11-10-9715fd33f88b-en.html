The Influence of Business Owner Leadership and Financial Literacy on Employee Productivity in MSMEs

Author: Martina Mundová

A new study published in Jurnal Ilmiah Accusi explores the connections between business owner leadership styles, financial literacy, and employee productivity in micro, small, and medium enterprises. We examine the insights derived from the available abstract and their practical application in digital environments and corporate website management.

Introduction

Small and medium enterprises form the backbone of economic stability across many regions, and their operations rely heavily on the quality of leadership. Successful management goes beyond routine operational tasks, requiring a systematic approach to human capital development. At WEBU, the long-term sustainability of small businesses is linked not only to efficient processes but also to high-quality internet presentation.

Interest in the factors influencing employee performance continues to grow steadily. Experts increasingly focus on the connection between managerial competencies of business owners and the internal literacy of their staff. This exact dynamic forms the core of a new academic paper analyzing data from the city of Medan.

What the study examined

Authors Markus Doddy Simanjuntak, Santi Rizki, and Ernita Siagian examine how business owner leadership influences employee productivity. The research targets both the direct effect of this influence and the indirect effect through financial literacy as a mediating variable in MSMEs in Medan City. Sources published under DOI: 10.36985/2c6j8996 define this specific sample in detail.

According to the available abstract, the study used an associative quantitative approach with a survey method. The research sample comprised 175 MSME employees working in various sectors such as culinary, fashion, handicrafts, and retail trade. Respondents were selected using purposive sampling techniques, and data were gathered through a 5-point Likert scale questionnaire.

Findings and interpretation

Based on the available abstract, data were analyzed using the Structural Equation Modeling approach based on Partial Least Squares with the help of SmartPLS 4. The authors state in the text that business owner leadership had a positive and significant effect on financial literacy, with a path coefficient of β = 0.412 at p < 0.001. Similarly, financial literacy showed a positive and significant effect on employee productivity at β = 0.358 with p < 0.001.

The available abstract further notes that business owner leadership had a direct positive and significant effect on employee productivity at β = 0.276 with p < 0.01. Mediation test results according to the text showed that financial literacy partially mediated this influence, yielding an indirect effect of 0.148 at p < 0.01 and a VAF value of 34.9%. These findings highlight the importance of pairing leadership capabilities with enhanced financial awareness.

Methodology and limitations

The methodological framework relies on quantitative data collection from 175 respondents using structured questionnaires. The application of the analytical tool SmartPLS 4 allows for reliable processing of complex relationships among variables within the conceptual model. However, it is vital to emphasize that this entire analysis is based exclusively on the limited scope of the published abstract.

Since the full text of the research article is unavailable, it is impossible to verify detailed data collection conditions, exact wording of survey questions, or the broader context of the research environment in Medan City. Consequently, conclusions must be approached with caution, avoiding premature generalizations to other markets and viewing causal interpretations strictly within the boundaries of the provided abstract.

WEBU practical proposal

From the perspective of editorial recommendations by WEBU, small businesses can leverage insights on leadership and financial literacy through digital transformation. Business owners should build dedicated internal sections or staff portals on their corporate websites. These client or employee zones can house educational modules focused on basic business finance, clear cost infographics, and understandable materials designed to boost work performance.

In web design and marketing, we recommend that small business owners integrate clear dashboards that visually display key performance indicators to employees. Transparent communication of company goals via a modern website supports a participative leadership style, reduces team uncertainty, and helps maintain long-term productivity regardless of physical workspace locations.

Conclusion

The presented study points to significant links between enterprise management styles, financial literacy, and resulting staff productivity. According to the available abstract, the data confirm that developing leadership skills should be accompanied by the systematic strengthening of employee financial awareness. These findings offer a valuable framework for ongoing managerial research activities within the small and medium enterprise sector.

Although the study yields interesting statistical metrics, applying its findings to everyday operations requires mindfulness regarding the limits stemming from the lack of full-text access. The WEBU editorial team recommends that business owners combine scientific insights with their own operational needs, develop digital tools, and maintain transparent team communication to achieve sustainable growth.
