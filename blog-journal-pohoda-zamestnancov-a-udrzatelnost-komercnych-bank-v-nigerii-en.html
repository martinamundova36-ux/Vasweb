Employee Well-Being and Organisational Sustainability in Nigerian Banks

Author: Martina Mundová

This study investigates how employee well-being and work-life balance influence the organizational sustainability of commercial banks in Rivers State, Nigeria. By analyzing key factors contributing to retention and operational efficiency, the research highlights the strategic importance of the human element in the banking sector, providing insights based on statistical correlations observed within the study's sample population.

Introduction

Organizational sustainability in the modern banking landscape depends not only on financial metrics but increasingly on the quality of employees' work lives. This study addresses the critical question of whether there is a measurable relationship between the psychological well-being of staff and a bank's ability to retain talent and maintain operational efficiency.

In the context of commercial banks in Rivers State, Nigeria, the author examines how specific aspects of employee well-being shape long-term institutional stability. According to the abstract, the study provides an empirical perspective on whether investments in human capital represent a strategic advantage for banking entities operating within a developing economic environment.

What the study examined

The research focused on identifying associations between two primary variables: psychological well-being and work-life balance, and organizational sustainability. According to the abstract, organizational sustainability was operationalized through two key indicators: employee retention rates and the overall operational efficiency of the banking institutions involved in the study.

The target population consisted of 484 employees from ten commercial banks operating in Rivers State, Nigeria. The study sought to determine if a statistically significant relationship exists between these variables, focusing on how the subjective perception of employee well-being correlates with objective organizational outcomes, such as the ability to retain talent and optimize internal processes within the demanding banking sector.

Findings and interpretation

According to the abstract, the study revealed a positive and statistically significant relationship between psychological well-being, work-life balance, and organizational sustainability. The findings suggest that banks where employees report higher levels of psychological well-being tend to exhibit higher retention rates and better operational efficiency, which aligns with theoretical assumptions regarding the importance of human capital in organizational success.

The significance of these findings lies in confirming that employee well-being is not merely an ethical consideration but a strategic factor influencing bank stability. According to the abstract, the author concluded that a relationship exists between these factors, leading to the recommendation that bank management should actively invest in mental health support programs and work flexibility to ensure the long-term viability of their institutions in the competitive market.

Methodology and limitations

The research employed a cross-sectional survey design, with a sample of 219 respondents determined using the Taro Yamane formula. Primary data were collected via a structured questionnaire. The validity of the instrument was determined through face and content validity, while reliability was tested using Cronbach's alpha, with a threshold of 0.7 set for the study. Data analysis was performed using the Pearson product-moment correlation coefficient.

It is important to note that the study is based on correlation, meaning that, according to the abstract, causality cannot be automatically assumed. Limitations include the geographic restriction to Rivers State and the specific banking sector context, meaning results may not be directly generalizable to other industries or different cultural and economic environments without further verification. The abstract does not provide details on potential confounding variables that might influence these outcomes.

Business relevance and limitations

Entrepreneurs can learn from this study that investments in employee well-being may have a direct impact on talent retention and operational stability. While the study focuses on banks in Nigeria, the principle that a satisfied employee is more productive and loyal is universally relevant. Entrepreneurs should assess whether their current processes support work-life balance or if imbalances are driving key personnel away.

For example, an entrepreneur could implement an anonymous satisfaction survey focused on perceived workload and stress to determine if there is a correlation between high turnover in specific teams and low levels of psychological well-being. However, it is essential to remember that correlation does not imply causation; turnover might also be driven by other factors like compensation or management quality, which the study did not explicitly analyze.

Conclusion

According to the abstract, the study confirms that a significant relationship exists between employee well-being and organizational sustainability in the banking sector. For managers, this serves as a clear signal that the human factor is a critical component of organizational strategy.

While the results are positive for banks in Rivers State, they serve as a prompt for reflection for entrepreneurs in other sectors rather than a universal manual. Future research should expand the scope to other industries and geographic regions to confirm the universal validity of these findings under different economic conditions.
