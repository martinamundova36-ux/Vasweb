Educational Management Explored: How School Leadership and Culture Shape Staff Satisfaction

Author: Martina Mundová

Current educational transformation demands strategic shifts in institutional leadership. A new study published in Journal La Edusci examines the extent to which instructional leadership and organizational culture affect public elementary school teachers' job satisfaction.

Introduction

The acceleration of educational transformation demands a strategic shift in school leadership and climate, yet teachers' job satisfaction is frequently overlooked amidst ongoing curriculum implementation challenges.

This editorial article examines a recent study published in the Journal La Edusci under DOI 10.37899/journallaedusci.v7i6.6030, exploring its core findings and foundational principles as presented in the accessible abstract.

What the study examined

According to the abstract, the study aimed to investigate the direct and simultaneous effects of principal instructional leadership and organizational culture on elementary school teachers' job satisfaction. Authors Sarki Sarki, Widya Kusumaningsih, and Nurkolis Nurkolis conducted the research in Larangan District, Brebes Regency.

Data were gathered from a population of 385 civil servant teachers across 52 state elementary schools. A sample of 196 teachers was selected via proportionate stratified random sampling using the Slovin formula.

Findings and interpretation

According to the abstract, the study employed an ex post facto correlational design using five-point Likert scale questionnaires exhibiting high internal consistency reliability above 0.95 alpha. Multiple linear regression and classical assumption tests were performed using SPSS 27.

The findings according to the abstract revealed that instructional leadership positively and significantly predicted teacher job satisfaction partially. Organizational culture also demonstrated a significant positive effect, while both predictors simultaneously explained a substantial portion of the variance in job satisfaction.

Methodology and limitations

When evaluating these outcomes, it is critical to account for limitations arising from the fact that only the publisher-supplied abstract was available. The complete full-text article was not independently verified, meaning detailed qualitative contexts remain unexamined.

Furthermore, the study relies on an ex post facto correlational design within a specific geographical population, which means associations must not be interpreted as definitive causation. These findings cannot be generalized directly to other regional or occupational settings without proper adaptation.

WEBU practical proposal

Although the primary study focuses on public education in Indonesia, WEBU editorial recommendations for small business owners and managers offer valuable parallels. Just as in schools, organizational commitment in small enterprises and marketing agencies heavily relies on supportive leadership.

Managers ought to implement coaching practices and cultivate inclusive, non-hierarchical work cultures utilizing internal communication tools and structured feedback mechanisms, which support workplace well-being and operational quality.

Conclusion

The study conclusion according to the abstract underscores the imperative for school principals to transition toward collaborative coaching and inclusive work cultures to sustain well-being. Empirical insights consistently validate the importance of empathetic leadership approaches.

These data serve as a useful prompt for anyone managing teams and seeking ways to enhance employee satisfaction and retention through better organizational culture and leadership.
