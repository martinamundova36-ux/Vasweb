Leadership Style and Engineers' Learning Agility: What Research Says About Algorithms and Safety

Author: Martina Mundová

A new study published in the Leadership & Organization Development Journal explores the relationship between leaders' growth mindset, psychological safety, and engineers' learning agility. We examine what the abstract reveals and what practical takeaways emerge for workplace and web project management.

Introduction

Modern team management in businesses, including digital agencies and development studios, faces challenges tied to rapid technological change. Alongside adopting new tools, the approach leaders take toward employee development and navigating change remains vital.

Fresh research published by Heungsok Cha, Hyun-Jung Kim, Youmi Lee, and Keunyoung Ji offers insight into how leaders' mindsets shape engineers and their ability to implement ideas in practice.

What the study examined

According to the abstract, the study examined whether a leader's growth mindset is associated with engineers' learning agility and idea implementation. An important part of the model was observing the role of team psychological safety as a mediator.

The authors also investigated whether this climate pathway is weakened when human resource processes are more intensely mediated by algorithmic and AI systems, utilizing a multi-source field study across 60 research and development teams over four months.

Findings and interpretation

According to the abstract, a leader's growth mindset was positively associated with team psychological safety, which in turn related positively to engineers' learning agility and idea-implementation counts.

On the other hand, higher algorithmic HRM intensity was, according to the abstract, negatively associated with psychological safety and attenuated the positive relationship between leader growth mindset and team climate.

Methodology and limitations

The methodology described in the abstract involved a multi-source, multi-wave data collection across 60 teams. Data collection took place across different waves, with leaders and engineers reporting various variables at separate time points.

Because we only have access to the publisher-supplied abstract without a verified full text, we cannot evaluate detailed statistical tests, effect sizes, or causal claims beyond what the authors explicitly state.

WEBU practical proposal

As an editorial recommendation for small businesses and web studios, integrating automated tools into management should not overshadow the human element and open team communication.

In web development and digital marketing, fostering an environment where teams do not fear mistakes and management supports experimentation yields better results than relying solely on automated performance metrics and algorithms.

Conclusion

The presented study highlights the importance of balancing modern technological HR systems with maintaining a safe, developmental team environment.

More details about the research and its connection to practice can be found in the original article under DOI: 10.1108/lodj-11-2025-1022.
