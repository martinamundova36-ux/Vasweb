Five business website problems and how to check them

By WEBU · Published 2026-11-03 · Updated 2026-11-04

Slovenčina

A business website should help visitors understand an offer and complete a specific task. Visual effects cannot fix a missing service description or a broken enquiry form. This WEBU checklist draws on accessibility and search documentation; it is not an experiment measuring lost customers.

Evidence and scope

W3C explains accessible page structure, forms and reflow on narrow screens. Google describes technical and content foundations for search discovery. These documents provide guidance, not a shared research sample or an estimated conversion effect.

Five areas to inspect

Check the entire journey from arrival to submission confirmation. Describe an observed obstacle rather than making a psychological judgement about the visitor.

  • Offer: can someone explain in their own words what the business does and whom it helps?
  • Mobile: can people read, navigate and complete forms without unwanted horizontal scrolling?
  • Performance: measure loading and responsiveness; distinguish lab results from real-user experience.
  • Credibility: are contacts, work examples and service descriptions accurate and verifiable?
  • Contact: do fields have clear labels, useful errors and visible submission confirmation?

Putting it into practice — WEBU proposal

Choose one important task and ask several people from the intended audience to complete it. Record specific failures and questions. Fix broken steps before experimenting with copy or layout. This combined process was not tested by the cited documentation.

Limits of the assessment

A small usability test cannot reliably estimate population-wide abandonment. A better performance score or a corrected error does not automatically establish more orders. Before comparing data, define the observation period, traffic sources and a successful enquiry.

Conclusion

Start with an obstacle you can demonstrate and reproduce. Assess task completion and enquiry quality, then evaluate business outcomes using your own data.
