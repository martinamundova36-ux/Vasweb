The Relationship Between Responsible Leadership, Financial Literacy, and Sustainable Financial Behavior Among Student Activists

Author: Martina Mundová

This research focuses on analyzing the factors influencing sustainable financial behavior among university students. According to the abstract, the study examines the relationships between responsible leadership, financial literacy, and financial self-efficacy. The findings highlight the importance of proper decision-making among youth in developing economies.

Introduction

Personal financial management represents a critical challenge for the younger generation, especially students involved in activist organizations. According to the abstract, high levels of theoretical understanding often fail to translate into rational, long-term oriented decisions. This discrepancy requires a more detailed examination of the factors shaping sustainable financial behavior within specific demographic groups.

Authors Aisyah Sulistiyani and Arum Pujiastuti directed their attention toward students in Indonesia. According to the abstract, the study seeks to understand how various forms of leadership and individual capabilities influence financial habits. The research responds to the need for supporting sustainable development goals through responsible approaches to fund management.

The published work offers a quantitative perspective on an issue relevant to both academia and policymakers. According to available abstract information, this represents an effort to connect organizational behavior with personal finances. The obtained insights aim to assist in designing more effective educational programs targeted at youth.

What the study examined

The subject of investigation was the impact of responsible leadership and financial literacy on sustainable financial behavior among students. According to the abstract, the researchers tested the direct effects of these variables while also verifying the mediating role of financial self-efficacy. The objective was to determine the extent to which these factors contribute to more responsible money management in a student environment.

The study focused in detail on a specific sample of 226 active organization students from six universities in East Java. According to the abstract, data were gathered using Likert-scale questionnaires and objective financial knowledge tests. This approach allowed the authors to obtain quantitative foundations for analyzing complex relationships among the investigated aspects.

The research design involved structural equation modeling utilizing the Partial Least Squares method, specifically via SmartPLS 4 software. According to the abstract, direct and indirect effects were tracked through statistical tests, evaluating beta coefficients and t-statistics. These methods provided a framework to assess the strength and significance of hypothesized relationships within the model.

Findings and interpretation

The research results according to the abstract demonstrated that responsible leadership, financial literacy, and financial self-efficacy exert positive and statistically significant effects on sustainable financial behavior. The authors report specific values for direct effects, where financial literacy, for example, displays a strong relationship with the resulting behavior. These findings indicate the complex nature of financial decision-making.

Beyond direct impacts, the study according to the abstract also confirmed the presence of partial mediation through financial self-efficacy. Responsible leadership and financial literacy likewise showed positive effects on self-efficacy itself. The overall coefficient of determination reached sixty point five percent, suggesting a substantial explanatory capacity of the model.

The stated conclusions according to the abstract imply that combining organizational leadership and financial knowledge forms a strong foundation for practical programs. The authors emphasize that the findings support the achievement of sustainable development goals, specifically regarding responsible consumption and production. However, interpretation stems exclusively from the available abstract text.

Methodology and limitations

The methodological approach relied on a quantitative explanatory study utilizing primary data from questionnaires and tests. According to the abstract, respondent selection was carried out through purposive sampling, which limits generalizability to the broader student population. Data analysis was conducted using advanced structural equation modeling in the SmartPLS 4 program.

A major limitation of the available information is the absence of the full-text study, preventing verification of questionnaire construction details and data collection procedures. According to the abstract, the research is restricted to a specific group of activist students in a single region of Indonesia. Consequently, causal conclusions cannot be unconditionally generalized to other cultural or economic settings.

The observed relationships in the study represent statistical associations and correlations that do not inherently prove unidirectional causality. According to the abstract, while the model exhibits a high coefficient of determination, other unexplored variables could have influenced the results. The reader must take these methodological boundaries into account when assessing the conclusions.

Business relevance and limitations

Entrepreneurs and managers can interpret the findings with great caution, as the study focuses on students rather than a commercial environment. According to the abstract, however, the work shows that education and self-confidence support responsible behavior. For firms, this may highlight the importance of supporting financial literacy within corporate training activities.

For example, in corporate incubator settings or training programs for young employees, management could test whether introducing mentoring focused on responsible leadership influences employee decision-making. It is necessary to measure changes in financial understanding and monitor participants' self-confidence levels. This procedure differs significantly from university conditions and requires independent validation in practice.

The applicability of insights to ordinary business practice is limited by the differing nature of student versus employee motivation. According to available data, this is academic research that does not provide a direct managerial playbook for running a business. Managers should consider the specific sample context and avoid hasty conclusions for commercial strategies.

Conclusion

The presented study provides an interesting perspective on the links between leadership, literacy, and financial behavior among young people. According to the abstract, the authors successfully demonstrated significant direct and indirect effects within the studied sample. These results enrich current knowledge regarding financial decision-making in developing nations.

Despite interesting statistical results, viewing the work within the context of its methodological limitations and missing full text is essential. According to the abstract, conclusions apply primarily to a specific group of Indonesian students. Further research should verify these relationships across other populations and distinct environments.

Overall, the study highlights the need to develop not only theoretical knowledge but also self-confidence and responsible leadership. According to the abstract, this contributes to sustainable development and better future planning. For the professional community, this serves as stimulating material for reflecting on educational programs.
