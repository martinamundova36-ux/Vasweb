What a business website costs: comparing quotations

By WEBU · Published 2026-11-03 · Updated 2026-11-04

Slovenčina

The same number of pages does not necessarily mean the same work. One quotation may include content preparation and testing; another may cover only a supplied template. Rather than invent a market average, this guide explains what to compare in a specific brief.

Professional foundations

W3C documents accessible structure and forms; Google describes search-discovery fundamentals. These requirements help define delivery scope. They provide neither WEBU prices nor a representative survey of Slovak suppliers, so we do not derive market averages from them.

What the scope should contain

Separate copy preparation, design, implementation, integrations and testing. For bookings or payments, describe failure behaviour and operational responsibility too. A defined scope is more useful than the vague promise of a complete website.

  • Content: unique pages, photography, languages and editorial review.
  • Design: template or custom components, navigation and mobile layout.
  • Functionality: forms, calendars, payments, integrations and required accounts.
  • Handover: verifiable tests, access and ownership of the domain and code.
  • Operation: hosting, domain renewal, support, updates and any paid services.

Putting it into practice — WEBU proposal

Give suppliers the same brief and request an itemised scope. Specify who supplies copy and photographs, the expected revision rounds and the acceptance criteria. This is an editorial comparison process, not experimental evidence of savings.

Limits of comparison

Prices depend on requirements and supplier terms. Cheaper does not necessarily mean worse, and a higher price does not establish quality. An indicative quotation is not a representative price index.

Conclusion

Compare delivery and future responsibilities, not just the total. Request a current quotation for your own brief and identify costs that continue after launch.
