Management Accounting Transformation in the Era of Artificial Intelligence: A Critical Review

Author: Martina Mundová

The contemporary business landscape is undergoing profound transformations driven by artificial intelligence. A new study published in 2026 analyzes how modern technologies impact core management accounting functions, including budgeting, cost analysis, and strategic decision-making.

Introduction

Artificial intelligence is penetrating all areas of business, significantly altering how companies process financial data. Management accounting faces the challenge of effectively integrating digital tools into daily operations without losing control over critical processes.

A new study by Nurchayati and colleagues focuses on the role artificial intelligence plays across four core pillars of management accounting. This article aims to provide a critical overview of these findings and highlight their real-world significance for enterprises.

What the study examined

According to the abstract, the research examined four core management accounting functions: cost analysis, budgeting, management control, and decision-making. The authors aimed to assess how digital tools influence efficiency within these areas.

The methodological foundation was a structured literature review. According to the abstract, the authors synthesized findings from systematic reviews, case studies, experiments, a dissertation, and professional reports, seeking to distinguish demonstrated predictive benefits from broader claims about organizational performance.

Findings and interpretation

According to the abstract, machine learning models can match or exceed human estimates in selected cost-estimation tasks when adequate historical data are available. In budgeting, machine learning can improve prediction accuracy, although predictive accuracy alone does not establish causal effects or guarantee appropriate resource allocation.

In management control, digital tools support more timely and anticipatory monitoring, although the available evidence does not always isolate the effect of AI itself. For decision-making, the usefulness of AI depends on data quality, model explainability, user trust, professional competence, and the preservation of human accountability.

Methodology and limitations

The authors utilized a synthesis of accessible literature, including case studies and experiments. However, it is important to note that the work was compiled based on available secondary sources and documentation.

According to the abstract, the review is limited by the absence of a complete database export and a study-specific PRISMA flow. It should therefore be read as a structured synthesis of accessible literature rather than a fully reproducible systematic review.

WEBU practical proposal

Based on editorial recommendations for the WEBU platform, small business owners, web design teams, and marketing professionals should approach artificial intelligence with caution. Before deploying tools for cost prediction or marketing budget creation, it is essential to ensure data cleanliness and quality, as poor inputs lead to misleading results.

In workplace management and web development practice, we recommend using AI as a support tool for professional judgment rather than a replacement for human oversight. Organizations must implement data governance, regularly evaluate model performance, and train staff to ensure full accountability for final decisions.

Conclusion

Artificial intelligence brings immense potential to management accounting, particularly in terms of more accurate cost estimations and timely monitoring. However, the study published under DOI 10.58764/j.im.2026.7.245 reminds us that technologies should serve as support for professionals rather than their replacement.

For successful integration into practice, rigorous data governance, continuous model evaluation, and the preservation of human accountability across all phases of strategic decision-making remain indispensable.
