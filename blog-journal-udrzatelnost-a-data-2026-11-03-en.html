What sustainability research databases can leave out

By WEBU · Published 2026-11-03 · Updated 2026-11-04

Slovenčina

A publication overview reflects selection and classification rather than the whole world. Ottaviani and Stahlschmidt (2026) examined another layer: how a language model represents Sustainable Development Goals when trained on abstracts selected by different databases.

What the study examined

This is a computational bibliometric analysis, not a survey of employees or customers. The common DOI-matched base of Web of Science, Scopus and OpenAlex contained 15,471,336 articles and reviews from 2015 to July 2023 meeting the metadata criteria. This is not a participant count or a claim that every record entered every training run.

The authors selected SDGs 4, 5, 8, 9 and 10. They fine-tuned DistilGPT-2 separately for database–goal combinations, used common prompts grounded in UN targets, and analysed generated terms with human assessment. They compared classifications and model outputs, not firms’ objective sustainability performance.

Main findings

Database classifications differed substantially. In the analysed model responses, the authors identified uneven representation of locations and topics, including weaker coverage of some aspects concerning disadvantaged groups. Their interpretation applies to the specific classification and modelling procedure.

This was not a direct audit of all current commercial AI assistants. A missing term does not necessarily mean missing research, and the model does not measure actual societal impact.

Putting it into practice — WEBU proposal

When gathering evidence for content or business strategy, record where sources came from and which filters selected them. Open the original for important claims rather than relying only on generated summaries. This is an editorial application, not a tested business intervention.

  • Record the database, date and selection method.
  • Compare a disputed claim with another independent source.
  • Distinguish publication counts from measured real-world outcomes.

Research limitations

Five selected goals do not cover all 17 SDGs. Historical snapshots are not today’s databases. Abstracts, language, prompts, the chosen model and human categorisation shape the output. Terminology may explain an absent expression; absence alone cannot establish that research does not exist.

Conclusion

Both databases and models mediate the view of evidence. A useful overview requires transparent selection and checks against original texts; an automated summary does not verify a claim by itself.
