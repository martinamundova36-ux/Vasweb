Digital Leadership and Corporate Performance in Tourism SMEs: What the New Research Shows

Author: Martina Mundová

A fresh study published in the Journal of Hospitality and Tourism Insights sheds light on how digital leadership relates to corporate performance in the tourism sector. In this WEBU editorial article, we examine what the researchers tracked according to the abstract, the associations identified, and the practical implications for small and medium-sized enterprises.

Introduction

The modern business environment in tourism places demanding technological expectations on managers. Small and medium-sized enterprises often operate under resource constraints, requiring efficient management and a strategic approach to innovation.

Industry professionals and academics alike are paying increasing attention to the role that managerial commitment and digital-age leadership play in organizational success. A recent scientific paper specifically targets this dynamic.

In this article, we dissect the published findings through a critical WEBU editorial lens, relying solely on data explicitly provided in the official abstract.

What the study examined

Authors Ahmed Mohamed Hasanein and Bassam Samir Al-Romeedy focused their research on the association between digital leadership and corporate performance. The investigation centered on tourism SMEs, specifically operating within travel agencies.

According to the abstract, the study draws theoretically on dynamic capabilities theory and the resource-based view. Digital leadership is regarded as a higher-order capability linked to developing and coordinating key internal factors.

The research team incorporated organizational learning, digital talent, and digital strategy as mediating capabilities, observing how they interconnect with overall organizational performance under constrained conditions.

Findings and interpretation

According to the abstract, the study identified positive associations between digital leadership and corporate performance. Furthermore, digital leadership was found to relate positively to organizational learning, digital talent, and digital strategy.

The three organizational capabilities also exhibited positive relationships with corporate performance. Each capability demonstrated a partial mediating role in the association between digital leadership and overall business performance.

These outcomes suggest that the link between leadership and performance is not entirely direct, but operates through process-oriented firm capabilities that help coordinate ongoing digital transformation.

Methodology and limitations

Methodologically, survey data were collected from 493 respondents. The participants consisted of senior managers and supervisory-level employees working in SME travel agencies.

To evaluate the proposed multiple-mediator model, the authors employed partial least squares structural equation modeling, representing a quantitative approach suited for testing complex variable relationships.

It is crucial to note a primary limitation: our review is based strictly on the publisher-supplied abstract, as the full text was not verified. Moreover, causation cannot be inferred from these associations, and findings apply to the studied sample without allowing automatic generalization to other national contexts without local research.

WEBU practical proposal

Building upon the study's theoretical foundations, the WEBU editorial team offers independent practical recommendations for small business owners, web designers, marketing specialists, and workplace managers. SMEs should systematically work on enhancing digital literacy across the entire team, rather than relying solely on executive leadership.

In web design and marketing, this means business owners should actively connect online presentation tools with employee training so that website maintenance and campaigns do not depend on a single individual. Establishing a clear digital strategy helps coordinate content creation and social media management.

At the workplace level, we recommend introducing regular knowledge sharing and encouraging staff to adopt new project management or communication tools, which streamlines daily operations and reduces stagnation risks.

Conclusion

The published study offers an insightful perspective on how digital leadership relates to firm performance in the tourism sector through organizational capabilities. Despite limitations stemming from relying solely on the abstract, it is evident that technological transformation requires a coordinated approach.

The interplay between leadership, strategy, and talent highlights that technology alone is insufficient; an organization's ability to learn and adapt is paramount. Managers should treat these insights as a prompt for the systematic development of internal capabilities.
