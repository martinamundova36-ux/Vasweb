When a website pays off: goals, costs and measurement

By WEBU · Published 2026-11-03 · Updated 2026-11-04

Slovenčina

A website needs a defined purpose: explain a service, support a booking or receive an enquiry. Assessing payback also requires costs and assumptions. Traffic volume and attractive design alone do not establish business value.

Professional foundations

Managerial accounting distinguishes revenue, variable and fixed costs, and break-even points (Franklin et al., 2019). Google explains search discovery, not guaranteed sales. This is an analytical guide with our own example, not a study of website effects across businesses.

What to measure and how to read the calculation

Separate visits, submitted forms, qualified enquiries and won business. Each metric answers a different question. A button click should not be labelled a sale.

WEBU illustration: a €600 initial cost divided by a €100 incremental contribution per job gives six jobs. This covers that initial amount in a simplified calculation; it is not a complete return-on-investment or profit calculation. It excludes ongoing costs, taxes, the time value of money and proof that the website caused those jobs.

Putting it into practice — WEBU proposal

Define one primary website task and how to observe it. For enquiries, track suitability and whether a job is subsequently won. Compare periods with known changes in advertising, seasonality and the offer.

  • List initial and ongoing costs.
  • Define a qualified enquiry.
  • Record traffic origin and business outcomes at an appropriate level.
  • Revisit assumptions when price, capacity or the offer changes.

Limits of the assessment

An improvement observed after launch is not necessarily caused by the website. Advertising, referrals or market conditions may contribute. Estimates based on few jobs are unstable; report the period and uncertainty.

Conclusion

Assess a website against a specific task and its overall costs. A simplified financial model helps frame questions, but it cannot guarantee investment recovery.
