Digital Twins in Construction: A Path to Sustainability and Well-being

Author: Martina Mundová

Integrating artificial intelligence into the building lifecycle delivers measurable results. We explore how technology is transforming not just construction, but the workplace itself.

Introduction

Artificial intelligence-driven digital twins (DTs) are increasingly promoted as a cornerstone for sustainable built asset management. However, empirical evidence regarding their full lifecycle application remains limited, particularly within emerging economy contexts.

The study by Anant Deogaonkar (2026) offers valuable insights through an analysis of the ‘Nirmitee Tower’ project in Central India. This article examines how the synthesis of technical and human-centric indicators influences modern construction projects.

What the study examined

The research focused on the integration of an AI-enabled DT system across the design, construction, and operational stages of the Nirmitee Tower, a green-certified commercial complex developed by a mid-sized construction firm.

A mixed-methods case study approach was adopted, triangulating evidence from system analytics, environmental performance metrics, semi-structured interviews, and post-occupancy surveys. Baselines were established using comparable pre-DT projects.

Findings and interpretation

According to the abstract, the deployment of the AI-DT system resulted in significant improvements. Project delivery time was reduced by 21%, and material waste decreased by 35.9%.

Regarding operational performance, the abstract reports a 28.3% reduction in annual energy use and a 21.1% reduction in water consumption. Furthermore, occupant comfort scores improved by 32%.

Methodology and limitations

The study provides a validated, multi-dimensional assessment framework for AI-DT performance. It is particularly notable for demonstrating full lifecycle DT integration within a small- to medium-scale enterprise context.

It is important to note that this is a case study of a single asset. As we do not have access to the full text, specific methodological limitations, such as the exact sample size of survey respondents, remain unspecified in this summary.

WEBU practical proposal

For small business owners and office managers, we recommend starting with the digitization of energy consumption data. Even without a full-scale digital twin, regular monitoring and data analysis can lead to savings that reduce operational overhead.

In terms of workplace design, we suggest leveraging employee feedback through regular satisfaction surveys. Correlating environmental data (such as temperature and lighting) with occupant experience is a vital first step toward creating a healthier, more productive workspace.

Conclusion

The study suggests that integrating AI and digital twins is not merely a technical endeavor but a tool for achieving UN Sustainable Development Goals. According to the abstract, the impact on efficiency and human well-being is significant.

The future of construction lies in bridging the gap between data and human needs. While technology implementation is demanding, the benefits in terms of reduced consumption and enhanced comfort are essential for modern business resilience.
