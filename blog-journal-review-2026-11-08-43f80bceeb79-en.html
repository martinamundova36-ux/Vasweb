Democratic Leadership and Nurse Performance: Insights from an Indonesian Hospital Study

Author: Martina Mundová

Human resource management and workplace communication styles fundamentally impact outcomes across industries, including healthcare and digital business. A new study published in 2026 examines how distinct management approaches influence staff efficiency. We examine these findings and their potential connections to small business management.

Introduction

Leadership style represents a vital organizational factor that directly influences employee performance and the overall quality of services provided in hospital settings. Effective management can improve work motivation, communication, collaboration, and professional responsibility among workers.

Despite theoretical insights, empirical verification remains essential to determine which specific approaches yield the best outcomes. This article reviews insights from recent research published by Tumbur Ojak Jonson Parningotan Sirait, Dwight Mahaputera Marulitua Hutapea, and Lyna M. N Hutapea.

This overview also serves as a point of reflection for small business owners and managers seeking proven ways to lead their teams toward better results, whether operating an e-commerce platform or providing digital services.

What the study examined

According to the abstract, this quantitative cross-sectional analytical study aimed to examine the relationship between authoritarian, democratic, and laissez-faire leadership styles and nurse performance. The authors conducted the research at Setio Husodo Hospital Kisaran in Indonesia.

Data collection took place between November and December 2025 using validated questionnaires. The study involved 95 nurses selected using purposive sampling from a total population of 125 nurses.

The applied methodology included univariate analysis, Chi-square tests, and ordinal logistic regression using SPSS software, with the level of statistical significance set at p less than 0.05.

Findings and interpretation

According to the abstract, most respondents were aged 26 to 35 years at 69.5 percent, while 90.5 percent held diploma-level nursing education and 55.8 percent possessed more than five years of work experience.

The results supported by statistical analysis indicated that democratic leadership showed a significant relationship with nurse performance, with a p-value equal to 0.002. In contrast, authoritarian leadership with a p-value of 0.294 and laissez-faire leadership with a p-value of 0.605 demonstrated no significant association.

Multivariate analysis according to the abstract confirmed that democratic leadership served as the strongest predictor of performance with a beta coefficient of minus 1.869 and a 95 percent confidence interval from minus 3.308 to minus 0.429 at p equal to 0.011. The authors recommend strengthening participatory approaches.

Methodology and limitations

When evaluating the published information, readers must consider the fact that only the publisher-supplied abstract is available, rather than the complete peer-reviewed text containing full methodological details.

A methodological limitation is the cross-sectional nature of the study, which prevents drawing direct causal relationships between leadership style and employee performance, indicating only statistical associations within the sampled group of 95 respondents.

Furthermore, the sample was drawn from a single hospital in Indonesia, meaning findings cannot be unconditionally generalized to other geographic areas, cultural settings, or industries such as technology firms in Europe.

WEBU practical proposal

From the perspective of editorial recommendations for small website operators and marketing agencies, the democratic approach can be translated into regular team involvement in editorial planning and UX design. Rather than directing tasks autocratically, encouraging web specialists and copywriters to openly discuss interface proposals and strategies proves beneficial.

In practice, e-commerce owners can introduce open weekly meetings where every team member can suggest user experience improvements, which observably increases work morale and final output quality regardless of the business sector.

This editorial proposal suggests combining managerial insights with digital feedback tools, helping companies maintain motivated employees and improve online services.

Conclusion

A summary of available data indicates that democratic leadership demonstrates a statistically significant association with nursing staff performance in the observed Indonesian facility, opening space for further professional discussions on effective healthcare management.

Despite limitations stemming from the availability of only an abstract and the cross-sectional nature of the research, the published data highlight the importance of open communication and employee participation in achieving organizational goals.

For readers of our WEBU portal, this offers a useful prompt to consider how to lead work teams and foster an environment that supports collaboration and professional responsibility.
