Pricing a service: costs, capacity and customer value

By WEBU · Published 2026-11-03 · Updated 2026-11-04

Slovenčina

More revenue does not necessarily mean more profit. Pricing a service requires knowing delivery costs, saleable capacity and what the customer actually receives. This guide uses basic managerial-accounting concepts; it does not identify the right price for a particular market.

Professional foundations

Franklin, Graybeal and Cooper (2019) distinguish contribution margin from profit and explain break-even analysis. These are textbook analytical concepts, not a customer-sample study. A calculation does not establish willingness to pay.

Costs and an illustrative calculation

Separate job-level variable costs from fixed costs for the period. Contribution margin is price minus variable costs; it first covers fixed costs, with any remainder contributing to profit. Value your own labour explicitly rather than leaving it invisible in the budget.

WEBU example: €40 direct costs, €120 valued labour and €30 allocated overhead total €190. This is an initial full-cost calculation, not a recommended market price. Do not count labour or overhead twice; tax effects depend on the circumstances.

Putting it into practice — WEBU proposal

Specify scope, revision rounds and handover terms in the quotation. Check capacity too: not every working hour is billable. Track estimated versus actual delivery time separately from marketing traffic.

  • Record costs, saleable hours and an allowance for corrections.
  • Define the deliverable and the problem it addresses.
  • Compare the completed job with its budget and revise assumptions.

Limits of the framework

Simplified models depend on a defined period and sufficiently stable costs and service mix. They do not automatically estimate demand, competition or non-payment risk. The illustration does not replace individual accounting or tax assessment.

Conclusion

A price should have an explainable basis in costs and scope. Customer acceptance is a separate question to investigate without promising guaranteed profit.
