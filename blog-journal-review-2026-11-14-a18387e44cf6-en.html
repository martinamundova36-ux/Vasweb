Digital Traceability in the Dairy Supply Chain: Study Insights and Editorial Proposals

Author: Martina Mundová

A new study published in the Asian Journal of Dairy and Food Research examines the enablers influencing digital traceability for food safety. This article reviews the findings from the abstract and links them to actionable tips for web design, marketing, and business owners.

Introduction

Digital traceability is gaining paramount importance in modern supply chains, ensuring enhanced transparency, information visibility, and food safety management.

In complex structures such as the Indian dairy sector, effective implementation depends on the interplay of multiple organizational, technological, and financial enablers.

Understanding the interrelationships among these factors is essential, serving as the core objective of the recent research led by Sandhya Jadhav and colleagues.

What the study examined

According to the abstract, the research aimed to identify the relative influence and interdependence of eight critical enablers for digital food safety traceability.

The authors approached implementation not merely as a software purchase, but as a complex alignment of human, financial, and regulatory dimensions.

The primary goal was to establish a structured model illustrating how these distinct driving forces condition and affect one another within a supply chain environment.

Findings and interpretation

According to the abstract, the study utilized an ISM-MICMAC approach, yielding a four-level hierarchical structure of interrelationships among the enablers.

Based on the provided abstract data, financial support and top management commitment emerged as foundational drivers supporting the entire system.

The findings suggest that without a solid baseline of investment and managerial backing, advanced layers of data transparency and standardization cannot be successfully built.

Methodology and limitations

According to the abstract, the methodology relied on an integrated Interpretive Structural Modelling framework combined with MICMAC classification.

The researchers developed the structural self-interaction matrix and performed reachability analysis using expert judgments and evaluations.

It is important to note that these insights stem exclusively from the publisher-supplied abstract, as the complete full-text article was not verified by the editorial team, representing a natural limitation.

WEBU practical proposal

These editorial recommendations extend beyond the study's scope to provide useful examples for small business owners, web designers, and marketing professionals focusing on transparency.

When managing a business website or e-shop, draw inspiration from the study's hierarchy: first secure a stable financial and technical foundation (hosting, security) before investing in advanced marketing tools.

In workplace management and internal workflows, ensure employee digital skills are developed systematically to facilitate the adoption of new software solutions and transparent data sharing.

Conclusion

Analyzing the enablers of digital traceability reveals that success relies not on a single technology, but on a coordinated blend of financing, leadership, and standards.

Although the study evaluated a specific sector, its structured view on priorities offers valuable inspiration for digital transformation across diverse industries.

Detailed information and complete contextual data can be found in the original publication via DOI: 10.18805/ajdfr.dr-2538.
