The Influence of Debt and Liquidity Policies on Financial Distress: An Analysis of PT Sepatu Bata Tbk

Author: Martina Mundová

This analysis examines the financial stability of PT Sepatu Bata Tbk over a thirteen-year period. The research focuses on how debt policy and liquidity influence the probability of financial distress, providing valuable insights into managing short-term liabilities and capital structure in the context of shifting market conditions and increasing pressure on corporate finances.

Introduction

Financial stability is a critical indicator of a company's long-term viability, especially in sectors facing intense competition. This study focuses on PT Sepatu Bata Tbk, which exhibited signs of deteriorating financial health during the observation period. The research analyzes the extent to which specific financial decisions, such as debt management and liquidity reserves, can predict or mitigate the risk of financial failure within a corporate environment.

Authors Fadillah and Ganar examine this issue through a quantitative analysis of financial statements. The goal is to understand the dynamics between leverage and the company's ability to meet short-term obligations. For entrepreneurs and financial managers, this analysis provides an important perspective on how seemingly standard financial metrics can signal deeper structural problems within an organization if they are not correctly monitored and managed during times of crisis.

What the study examined

The study focused on the financial statements of PT Sepatu Bata Tbk for the period from 2013 to 2025. According to the abstract, the primary objective was to analyze the effect of debt policy and liquidity policy on the firm's financial distress. The researchers sought to determine whether these factors act on financial stability partially or simultaneously, utilizing a quantitative approach and descriptive method to process secondary data from accounting records.

Specifically, debt policy was measured using the Debt to Equity Ratio (DER), and liquidity was evaluated through the Current Ratio (CR). Financial distress was quantified using the Altman Z-Score, a recognized tool for predicting bankruptcy risk. The study aimed to identify whether changes in these indicators directly correlate with the negative development of the company's financial health, which is crucial for understanding the mechanisms leading to financial distress over the thirteen-year observation period.

Findings and interpretation

According to the abstract, the results show that the Debt to Equity Ratio (DER) has no statistically significant effect on financial distress (p = 0.207). Conversely, liquidity measured by the Current Ratio (CR) has a positive and statistically significant effect on financial distress (p < 0.001). These findings suggest that for the company studied, the ability to manage liquidity is a more critical factor for maintaining financial stability than the level of debt itself.

Simultaneous analysis, according to the abstract, confirmed that DER and CR together significantly affect financial distress (F = 23.597; p < 0.001), with an Adjusted R Square reaching 0.790. This indicates that these two factors can explain a large portion of the variability in the company's financial health. In conclusion, managing current assets and short-term liabilities is essential for preventing financial failure, which underscores the importance of operational financial planning within the enterprise.

Methodology and limitations

The research utilized a quantitative methodology based on thirteen years of data from the financial statements of PT Sepatu Bata Tbk. The statistical methods employed included descriptive statistics, classical assumption tests, and multiple linear regression. While this approach provides a clear picture of the company's historical development, it is important to note that the study is limited to one specific firm, which restricts the generalizability of the results to other sectors or market conditions.

Limitations also include the fact that the analysis is based solely on secondary accounting data. According to the abstract, it is unclear what external economic factors, such as changes in consumer behavior or macroeconomic shocks, may have influenced the results. Furthermore, as this is a case study of a single company, the findings may not apply to other enterprises with different capital structures or in different geographic regions, requiring caution when interpreting the findings in a broader context.

Business relevance and limitations

Entrepreneurs should pay increased attention to liquidity management, as the study suggests its impact on financial health is more significant than the level of debt. Maintaining sufficient cash and effectively managing short-term liabilities are key to survival. For example, an entrepreneur could regularly test their Current Ratio (CR) and simulate the impact of delayed payments from customers on their ability to meet obligations to identify potential risks of financial distress early.

However, it must be remembered that these findings are specific to PT Sepatu Bata Tbk and may not be universally applicable. An entrepreneur should view these indicators as part of broader risk management, not as the sole indicator of success. It is recommended to combine quantitative financial analysis with qualitative assessment of the market environment, as financial statements reflect past events, while strategic decisions must account for future challenges and changing industry conditions.

Conclusion

The study by Fadillah and Ganar emphasizes that liquidity is a key pillar of a company's financial stability. Although debt is important, a firm's ability to pay short-term obligations proved to be the decisive factor in predicting financial distress. For managers, this means that the priority should be maintaining a healthy liquidity position, which provides the necessary flexibility to address unforeseen financial pressures and ensures business continuity during difficult periods.

The research provides a valuable perspective on financial management through the analysis of a specific case. Although the results are limited to one company, they highlight the need for systematic monitoring of financial indicators. For entrepreneurs, it is a reminder that financial health is not a static state but a dynamic process that requires constant attention and adaptation to both internal and external conditions to avoid long-term problems and ensure business stability.
