Digitalizing Service Records in Automotive Workshops: A FIFO System Analysis

Author: Martina Mundová

This study analyzes the implementation of a web-based service record system at Hexen Garage. The research focuses on utilizing the First In, First Out (FIFO) method for repair management and evaluates the software's functionality through black-box testing. The goal is to determine how digital tools influence transparency and operational coordination in a workshop setting, according to the provided abstract.

Introduction

Traditional service record management in the automotive industry often faces limitations regarding information accessibility and operational coordination. According to the abstract, this study focuses on developing a web-based system designed to replace outdated procedures at Hexen Garage. The objective is to create a digital environment that enhances service transparency and streamlines communication between customers, mechanics, and administrative staff.

The research concentrates on integrating the FIFO mechanism, which ensures that service requests are processed in the order they were received. This approach has the potential to reduce chaos in the workshop and improve overall workflow organization. According to the abstract, the authors utilized the Waterfall methodology for system development, emphasizing functional integrity and practical usability within a real-world automotive workshop environment.

What the study examined

The study examined the development and evaluation of a web-based information system for Hexen Garage. According to the abstract, researchers designed a platform that integrates customer data management, vehicle records, repair progress monitoring, and chronological queue management using the FIFO method. The system was designed for three specific user roles: administrators, mechanics, and customers, aiming to cover all key aspects of the service process.

The research design was based on the Waterfall methodology, which is typical for linear software development. According to the abstract, data were collected through observations, interviews, and literature reviews. This approach was intended to ensure that the system reflected the actual needs of the operation. The authors aimed to create a unified application that connects administrative tasks with the technical execution of repairs, focusing on the functional validation of the entire system.

Findings and interpretation

The results of functional testing, known as Black Box Testing, yielded mixed findings. According to the abstract, eight out of ten defined scenarios were successfully met, representing an 80 percent success rate. These scenarios included basic system functions such as data management and repair monitoring. However, 20 percent of the scenarios failed, specifically regarding vehicle deletion validation and administrator access restrictions.

These findings suggest that while the system provides a technical foundation for structured management, it requires further refinement before full operational deployment. According to the abstract, the study's significance lies in the integration of digital records and online repair monitoring. Although the system offers a promising framework for improving information flow, failures in critical security and validation functions highlight the need for more rigorous testing and development before the system is considered fully functional for commercial use.

Methodology and limitations

The study's methodology involved a combination of qualitative analysis and quantitative functional testing. According to the abstract, the Waterfall model was used, which is suitable for projects with clearly defined requirements. Data collection occurred through observations and interviews at Hexen Garage. However, this approach is limited by the specific environment of a single workshop, meaning the results may not be automatically generalizable to other types of automotive workshops or different operational models.

A key limitation is that the study relies on Black Box Testing, which focuses on inputs and outputs without deeper analysis of the internal code. According to the abstract, the failure of 20 percent of the scenarios indicates that the system is not ready for live operation in its current form. Furthermore, the absence of information regarding the long-term impact on productivity or customer satisfaction means the study provides only a technical view of development, not a comprehensive economic analysis of benefits.

Business relevance and limitations

Entrepreneurs can learn from this study the importance of a systematic approach when digitalizing internal processes. According to the abstract, it is crucial that software solutions for workshops include not only record-keeping but also transparent queue management. For example, if you operate a workshop, you could test the introduction of a digital FIFO system to track waiting times. Measure the time from vehicle intake to repair completion and compare it with the previous state, while ensuring thorough validation of employee access rights.

An important lesson is the necessity of rigorous testing before a full launch. According to the abstract, even a 20 percent error rate in key functions, such as data deletion or access rights, can threaten operational integrity. Therefore, when introducing any new system, an entrepreneur should demand not only a functional demonstration from the supplier but also certified results of security and data integrity tests to avoid unexpected operational complications after implementation.

Conclusion

The study provides a valuable perspective on the development of a digital system for Hexen Garage. According to the abstract, the integration of the FIFO mechanism and online repair monitoring is a step toward more modern workshop management. Although the system achieved an 80 percent success rate during testing, the identified shortcomings in administration and data validation suggest that the technical solution requires further development and optimization before real-world deployment.

For entrepreneurs, the main takeaway remains the need for a balanced approach between innovation and reliability. According to the abstract, this research is primarily beneficial as a technical foundation that points the way toward better transparency. However, future successful deployment depends on fixing the identified errors and ensuring system robustness, which is a necessary prerequisite for any successful digital transformation in the automotive service sector.
