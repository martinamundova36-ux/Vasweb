Pricing tests: separating change from random variation

By WEBU · Published 2026-11-03 · Updated 2026-11-04

Slovenčina

Sales may rise without a price change, for example because of advertising or seasonality. A simple before-and-after comparison may therefore miss the price effect. This methodological guide draws on NIST; it does not report findings from the originally cited conference paper.

What the methodological source establishes

The NIST/SEMATECH handbook explains objectives, variable selection, randomised designs and blocking of nuisance factors. It is methodological documentation, not one customer-sample study. A specific pricing design also depends on capacity, ethics and possible interference between groups.

Association, prediction and causal questions

An observed sales difference between periods is an association. A suitable randomised experiment may support a causal interpretation within its conditions. A model predicting sales does not, by itself, identify the effect of changing price.

Blocking groups comparable conditions; randomisation governs assignment within the design. Cluster or time-switching designs need to account for dependence and possible carryover. No universal number of orders makes every pricing test reliable.

Putting it into practice — WEBU proposal

Write down the question and decision rules before launch. Consider whether the data and operating conditions can support a meaningful experiment at all. This checklist is not a reported finding from one study.

  • Choose a primary metric, such as contribution margin rather than order count alone.
  • Define comparison conditions and nuisance factors.
  • Plan duration, the smallest relevant difference and uncertainty analysis.
  • Do not stop early merely because an interim result looks favourable.

Limitations and the original citation

The Ferrari Ortiz and colleagues conference record is dated 6 November 2026 in Crossref. Neither its full text nor abstract was available for comparison; sample and findings remain unverified. We identify it transparently as an unchecked original source, not evidence that temporal pricing switchbacks work. Scientific assessment of that paper remains pending.

Conclusion

A useful pricing test requires an appropriate comparison and respect for uncertainty. When design or data are insufficient, the appropriate conclusion is that a difference has not established an effect.
