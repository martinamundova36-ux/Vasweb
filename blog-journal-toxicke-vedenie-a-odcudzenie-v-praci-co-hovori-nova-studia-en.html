Abusive Supervision and Work Alienation: Insights from New Research

Author: Martina Mundová

The relationship between supervisors and employees is a cornerstone of workplace health. A new study in the Journal of Education, Humanities and Social Sciences explores how toxic management behaviors contribute to employee alienation.

Introduction

Work alienation is a negative psychological state where employees feel disconnected from their tasks and organization. This state frequently leads to decreased job satisfaction and higher turnover, posing significant challenges for modern management.

The study by Yundi Sun (2026) investigates the dynamics between abusive supervision and this state of alienation, examining the mediating role of job stress and the moderating role of organizational support.

What the study examined

According to the abstract, the research examined how abusive supervision affects work alienation, with job stress serving as a mediator and perceived organizational support as a moderator of this relationship.

The theoretical framework is grounded in social exchange theory and conservation of resources theory, which help explain why negative leadership behaviors trigger feelings of isolation among staff members.

Findings and interpretation

According to the abstract, the results show that abusive supervision has a significant positive effect on work alienation. Job stress, according to the abstract, partially mediates this relationship, suggesting that stress is a primary mechanism through which negative leadership impacts an employee's psyche.

Furthermore, according to the abstract, perceived organizational support weakens the positive effect of abusive supervision on work alienation. This indicates that a strong culture of support can act as a protective buffer for employees.

Methodology and limitations

According to the abstract, the study was conducted via a questionnaire survey among 240 employees of Chinese enterprises, with 230 valid responses analyzed using SPSS 25.0 and AMOS 24.0 software.

It is important to note that this is a summary based on the abstract, and the full text was not reviewed. A limitation is the geographic specificity of the sample, meaning these findings cannot be automatically generalized to different cultural or workplace environments.

WEBU practical proposal

Editorial recommendation: For small business owners, building a transparent culture where feedback is bidirectional is essential. If your company website includes an internal portal, ensure there are anonymous channels for reporting workplace concerns.

In team management and marketing, we recommend prioritizing 'perceived support'. Regular recognition of work and clear expectations reduce stress, which, according to the abstract, directly helps prevent employee alienation.

Conclusion

Yundi Sun's study provides valuable insights into workplace psychology. Although the research is limited to a specific context, it emphasizes that management behavior is not merely a matter of personal style, but a critical factor affecting organizational stability.

Investing in a healthy work environment and actively supporting employees appear, based on the findings, to be the most effective tools for preventing alienation and retaining talent within a team.
