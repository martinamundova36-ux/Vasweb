Digital Diplomacy and Translocal Identity: New Challenges for Global Mobility

Author: Martina Mundová

The contemporary global work environment introduces new forms of international collaboration, with virtual international assignments replacing traditional physical relocation. A new study published in the Journal of Global Mobility applies Identity Work Theory to examine how professionals negotiate professional legitimacy from their home base.

Introduction

The growing reliance of multinational corporations on virtual international assignments raises new questions about how professionals maintain their status without physical relocation. Traditional expatriation models are often replaced by remote work arrangements where digital presence and cross-cultural connectivity play a critical role.

According to the abstract of the study by Isabel Barbosa and Elizabeth Real de Oliveira in the Journal of Global Mobility (DOI: 10.1108/jgm-06-2026-0070), attention focuses on how employees without physical relocation negotiate professional legitimacy, belonging, and trust. The authors draw on Identity Work Theory and frame these efforts as translocal identity work.

This WEBU Journal editorial article provides an overview of these findings, emphasizing limitations arising from the availability of the abstract alone. We also connect these academic insights with practical recommendations for smaller teams, web design, and virtual workplace management.

What the study examined

According to the abstract, the qualitative study focused on three multinational corporations operating across technology, consulting, and manufacturing sectors. The research analyzed the experiences of employees formally assigned to manage cross-border responsibilities without planned relocation from their home base.

The methodological approach described in the abstract involved conducting thirty semi-structured interviews with these virtually assigned workers. Visual network elicitation was used to prompt discussion of assignment-related relationships, and data were analyzed using a team-based thematic approach.

It is important to note that these details stem exclusively from the publisher-supplied abstract, and the complete study text has not been independently verified. Consequently, limitations regarding the broader data context and the exact scope of the methodology must be taken into account.

Findings and interpretation

According to the abstract, the study highlights three interrelated themes of translocal identity work: translocal professionalism, digital diplomacy, and perceived psychologically safe inclusion. The first theme captures the global professional identity that employees sought to sustain while remaining locally based.

The second theme, digital diplomacy, involves deliberate relational labor according to the abstract, aimed at building and sustaining cross-cultural trust through digital channels. The third theme addresses leadership-mediated conditions supporting or constraining open voice and authentic participation.

The abstract's conclusions suggest that symbolic and social proximity can support professional legitimacy without relocation, yet persistent marginality and relational strain indicate that these efforts remain contingent on organizational and leadership support. The authors introduce the concept of translocal identity work, contributing to global mobility research.

Methodology and limitations

The methodology of this research, as outlined in the abstract, relies on a qualitative multiple case study across three specific sectors. The use of thirty interviews and visual network elicitation provides deep insight into the experiences of selected employees.

A critical limitation of this editorial piece is that we work solely with the publisher abstract. The full text of the study was not verified, meaning we lack details on potential statistical correlations or deeper qualitative nuances that the complete text might contain.

Furthermore, it must be noted that findings derived from multinational corporations cannot be unconditionally generalized to any other setting, particularly local conditions in different regions or entirely distinct small business structures.

WEBU practical proposal

For small business owners and digital team managers, the study's findings offer valuable parallel insights. When creating web projects and managing remote marketing campaigns, it is crucial to build a transparent digital environment that fosters trust and open communication among team members.

In web design and website management, this translates to creating intuitive communication interfaces and clear sections for clients and teams alike, replacing personal contact. Similarly to the concept of digital diplomacy, one must consciously invest in relationships through regular online meetings and clearly defined processes.

In remote workplace management, we recommend implementing measures to support psychological safety so that employees do not feel isolated. Maintaining regular contact and respecting diverse cultural backgrounds helps overcome the feelings of marginalization mentioned in the study.

Conclusion

Virtual international assignments represent a modern challenge for organizations relying on digital collaboration instead of physical relocation. The examined concept of translocal identity work points out that professional legitimacy and trust are attainable, but they require systematic effort.

According to the abstract of Barbosa and Real de Oliveira's study, these efforts remain sensitive to the level of support provided by leadership and the organization. Without adequate backing, relational strain and feelings of exclusion may occur.

WEBU Journal advises managers to approach digital communication strategically. Combining theoretical insights with practical measures in corporate culture and digital tools is key to successful remote collaboration.
