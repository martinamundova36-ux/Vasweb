Managing the Digital Shift: Information Systems and AI in EU Firms

Author: Martina Mundová

This study analyzes the integration level of management information systems and artificial intelligence across enterprises in 27 EU member states. According to the abstract, the research identifies significant gaps in digital maturity between regions and emphasizes the critical role of population digital skills in technology adoption, providing valuable insights into Romania's position within the European digital landscape.

Introduction

Digital transformation is a necessity for modern enterprises, rather than just a strategic advantage. This study focuses on how European firms integrate advanced technologies such as cloud computing, business intelligence, and artificial intelligence. According to the abstract, the research aims to map the state of digital integration across 27 EU member states and identify factors that influence the ability of enterprises to effectively utilize these tools in their daily operations.

Darko Shuleski's research provides a comparative view of the technological readiness of the European business sector. It focuses on enterprises with ten or more employees, analyzing six key indicators of digital integration. According to the abstract, the study provides an empirical basis for understanding why some countries lag in innovation adoption and what the main obstacles are for enterprises operating in less developed digital ecosystems within the European Union.

What the study examined

The study examined the integration of six specific technologies: enterprise resource planning (ERP), customer relationship management (CRM), business intelligence, cloud computing, artificial intelligence, and data analytics. According to the abstract, these indicators were analyzed as a single coherent dimension in enterprises with ten or more employees across 27 EU countries. The data are derived from Eurostat surveys on information and communication technology usage in enterprises for the period 2019–2025.

Beyond the rate of technology adoption, the research focused on the correlation between digital integration and macroeconomic factors. According to the abstract, the authors examined the relationship between technological maturity and indicators such as labor productivity, income per capita, and the digital skills of the population. The study specifically focuses on the position of Romania, which ranked 26th on the composite index, occupying the last position in artificial intelligence in every observed year.

Findings and interpretation

The research identified a clear leading group consisting of four countries, while six Central and Eastern European countries, including Romania, make up the low-adoption group. According to the abstract, the gap between Romania and the EU aggregate in the observed technologies has actually widened over time. This trend is present across all size classes, including large enterprises, which suggests a systemic problem in accessing innovation within that specific region.

An interesting finding is that technology integration is more closely associated with the digital skills of the population than with income per capita. According to the abstract, the association between digital integration and labor productivity is only moderate. These results suggest that the mere availability of technology is insufficient; the key factor for successful digital transformation is the ability of employees and managers to effectively master these tools and implement them into business processes in real time.

Methodology and limitations

The study's methodology included descriptive statistics, sigma and beta convergence analysis, a composite integration index, and hierarchical cluster analysis. According to the abstract, these methods were used to process data from Eurostat surveys. The study relies on quantitative data that allows for cross-country comparison; however, it is limited to enterprises with ten or more employees, meaning that micro-enterprises and sole traders are not included in the analysis.

The study's limitations stem from the nature of secondary data and the focus on the macro level. According to the abstract, the study does not provide an in-depth qualitative view of why specific firms fail in AI adoption. As this is an observational study, direct causal conclusions regarding the impact of technology on productivity cannot be drawn. Furthermore, findings regarding Romania cannot be automatically generalized to other Central and Eastern European countries without further examination of specific national conditions.

Business relevance and limitations

Entrepreneurs should view digital transformation not as a software purchase, but as an investment in their team's skills. According to the abstract, the correlation between digital skills and technology integration is stronger than the correlation with economic wealth. For an entrepreneur, this means that even in an economically less developed environment, a firm can gain a competitive advantage if it focuses on training employees in data analytics and modern information systems.

For example, if an entrepreneur considers implementing an AI system for customer support automation, they should first measure the current level of their team's digital skills. If they find that skills are low, training investment must precede technology purchase. The entrepreneur should monitor whether productivity actually increases after the tool is introduced, while keeping in mind that, according to the abstract, this link is only moderate on a broader scale and depends on many other factors.

Conclusion

The study provides a valuable overview of the digital divide within the EU and emphasizes that technological progress is not automatic. According to the abstract, it is essential for enterprises and policymakers to pay greater attention to the development of digital skills, which are the primary prerequisite for the successful integration of modern systems. Without this foundation, advanced technologies like AI remain underutilized, which hinders the growth of labor productivity and competitiveness in the European market.

Darko Shuleski's findings serve as a reminder that digital transformation is a long-term process requiring a strategic approach. According to the abstract, the situation in low-adoption countries like Romania is a warning sign for managers not to underestimate education and the systematic integration of technology. For entrepreneurs, it is crucial to understand that success depends on a combination of the right tools and the readiness of human capital, which is a universal lesson for any market within the European Union.
